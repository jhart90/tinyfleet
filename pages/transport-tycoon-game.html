A transport tycoon game you drive yourself

Every transport empire starts with one vehicle and an empty ledger. TinyFleet is a management game played from the driver’s seat: you deliver, put the money back into the company, and build something the towns prefer over the rival firms on the same roads.

From one van to a transport company: vehicles, drivers, a headquarters, rival firms and one shared world.

The company

  • Vehicles. More than 70, bought at dealers whose stock and prices change: vans, buses, tippers and lorries, then trains, ferries and a paddle steamer, then helicopters and jets. Tech trees for land, sea and air open the dealers that sell the big ones.
  • Drivers. Hire a crew, give each vehicle its standing orders, and move drivers and vehicles between slots on the depot board.
  • Headquarters. Grade your HQ up from a house to a tower. Each grade opens more fleet slots.
  • The Books. A ledger of where every dollar came from and went, by day and by line of business.
  • Property. Buy a building in any town and take a cut of every delivery made at its door, including your rivals’.
  • Territory. Hold a town’s trade and it pays diamonds every day, to spend on the tech trees and on lobbying towns to build what your fleet needs.

Things to optimise

The loads on offer depend on what each town makes and wants, and the best run is rarely the nearest one. A fleet can combine several loads into one trip, heavy freight pays more in a vehicle with the hold for it, and some jobs are only offered to the right kind of vehicle.

Fleet Metrics puts fourteen numbers side by side, you against every rival you have met: net worth, revenue, deliveries, on-time rate, fleet size, towns held. When the roads are the problem, build better ones: your own roads, bridges, tunnels and railway lines go where the generated ones fall short.

The rivals

Computer-run companies in their own liveries buy vehicles, hire drivers, court the towns and take the loads you wanted. Villages start firms of their own. The Standings show every firm’s share of each town, and the local newspaper reports who is winning.

When you are big enough, you can stop competing and buy them: a single depot, or the whole firm.

Drive, or manage

You are not watching this from a map. You drive the lead vehicle yourself, and your fleet can fall in behind you and lend you its holds for one big run. When you would rather manage, hand the wheel to a driver and watch the money come in. Hired drivers keep working while you do something else.

Against other players

All of this happens in one shared online world. Other players’ companies are on the same roads, and the leaderboards compare you with all of them. You can also share a company with friends.

Questions and answers

Is TinyFleet a tycoon game or a driving game?

Both. You build and manage a transport company, and you can drive any vehicle in it yourself.

Can I just manage and let drivers do the work?

Yes. Hired drivers work to their orders on their own, and you can spend your time on the fleet, the books and the map.

Are there rival companies?

Yes. Computer-run firms compete for the same towns, and other players’ companies share the same world.

What can I build?

Roads, bridges, tunnels, railway track and stations, and buildings from plans you own.

Is it free?

Yes. It is free to play in a browser, with free apps for Windows and Mac.
