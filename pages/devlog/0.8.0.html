TinyFleet 0.8.0: Coast to Coast

Version 0.8.0 · 10 November 2026

0.8.0 is the biggest TinyFleet update yet. The online world begins again, and it's a much bigger one: two thirds of it continent now, crossed by interstates and pylon lines, with farm belts, named forests, great bridges and castles out in the country, and a walled stronghold for each faction. Every vehicle has work of its own, your buildings make goods, you can lay your own railway, and there are rushes to run, toppers and crowns to earn, and a whole social side to the game.

🌍 The world begins again

0.8.0 makes a new online world, so everyone starts in a fresh spot on it. Your career comes with you:

  • Your fleet keeps its vehicles, workshop parts, names, livery and cash, and your tech, unlocks and faction standing.
  • Every building you owned is refunded at the full price you paid for it. Your HQ moves to the most like building near where it stood, bought for you out of that refund.
  • Everything you spent building into the old world (roads, bridges, track, stations, boost pads and earthworks) is refunded too, so you can build again.
  • Your achievements and Passport stamps all carry over.

New players get going faster too: the server picks out a good start ahead of time, so the first load is the only load.

🗺️ Coast to coast

  • More continent, fewer islands. About two thirds of the world is mainland now, and a third islands.
  • Interstates. Divided, limited-access highways run across the continents county to county, straight and at 45°, with long sweeping bends and decks over whatever they cross. Each county has one or two diamond interchanges, and where two interstates meet there's a link road between them. Through-traffic uses them, and a trip over a county line takes the interstate when it's quicker.
  • Signs over the highway. Exit boards hang from gantries over the carriageway, some with amber message boards, and every paved road into a county passes its welcome sign.
  • Pylons. Lattice transmission lines march out from each county's power station or biggest town and on over the county line.
  • Long bridges. Bridging from one county to the next over the water is easy, for every kind of road, and the builder and the earthworks tool both reach over county lines.

🌾 Out in the country

  • Farm belts. Inland counties in the middle of the continent are cut into a grid of fields, one crop each (wheat, maize, sunflowers…), with gravel section roads and farmsteads.
  • Named woods. Twelve kinds of forest are places of their own, with a name on the map: aspen groves, maple woods, fir and larch forests, cypress swamps, mangroves, eucalyptus, burned woods with their snags and fallen logs, drowned woods standing in the water and more.
  • Great crossings. Now and then a county's most dramatic bridge is built as a famous kind and named: a steel deck arch or a concrete arch over a gorge, a tall viaduct, or a through-truss over a wide river.
  • Castles. A castle school with its towers and great hall, and baronial castles of pink-grey stone with round turrets.
  • A desert ksar. A walled village of earth towers in the dunes.
  • Wildlife. Hawks and eagles circling, geese flying in a V, deer that bolt for the trees, foxes at dusk, and the odd bear or mountain lion.
  • Roads along the shore are kept out of the water, and water that drops a step pours down a chute instead of ending in a sheer wall.

🏰 Faction strongholds

Each of the ten factions now has a headquarters of its own: a great walled complex, each one laid out differently, with buildings you won't see anywhere else. There's one of each within ten counties of the middle of the online world, and only about one in four hundred counties further out.

  • Ten of them:
    • The Constabulary's college, a quadrangle round its parade square.
    • Villa Caruso, up a cypress-lined drive on a hilltop.
    • The Citadel, a star fort with a domed chancery.
    • Site Null, a fenced lakebed with a saucer in its hangar.
    • Federation House, which you enter through an arch in the headquarters itself.
    • The General Post Office, with an in gate and an out gate.
    • The Army's walled cantonment beside its air force base.
    • Lark Studios, with a backlot street you can drive.
    • Corran Harbour, round its walled basin.
    • Vane House, with a helipad on top of its broadcast tower.
  • Papers, please. Only players a faction counts Honoured are let in. Anyone else is stopped at the gate, told why, and turned round.
  • Closed skies. Nothing you fly can land at a stronghold below Honoured: helicopters can't set down there, and its runways won't take you.
  • Stronghold cargo. Once you're Honoured, a stronghold sends out its own valuable loads, and others come in to it from the faction's buildings nearby. They pay well and raise your standing more than ordinary faction work. You have to carry them yourself; hired drivers can't.
  • The Oath goes through the gates. Every faction's Oath now begins with a summons to its stronghold, has an errand there, and ends with the swearing there.
  • The Factions tab shows where each stronghold is, and driving into all ten earns a new achievement, Through the Gates.

🏙️ Towns and coasts

  • Rich enclaves. Estates in ten styles, behind a gatehouse, round a golf course, up a hillside or on the edge of a megalopolis.
  • University campuses round a quad, and big zoo parks. A town zoo's elephants, giraffes and penguins wander their pens.
  • Beach piers. Warm coastal towns get a fishing pier, a pleasure pier or a grand pier out over the sea, with lifeguard towers on the sand.
  • Headland parks. A big coastal city's headland kept as parkland, with its old officers' row, a gun battery and a lookout.
  • Streets with character. Five new kinds of lamp post, two to a town, so main streets and side streets look different, with wires along the country roads' poles. Pedestrians are little figures with a body and a head.
  • Read it from the air. Thirty kinds of building wear a big icon on the roof, and every building is called what it looks like: a shop with a key on its roof is a Locksmith.
  • The town card. Click a town for its trade, standing, works, culture and what's on, and rank every town you've seen in the register.
  • Shared culture. A town's culture is the same for every player: bring Tokyo goods into Port Alder and every fleet sees Port Alder grow more Tokyo.

🚜 Every vehicle has a job

  • Working vehicles have missions only they are offered: the Dozer knocks down eyesores, the Medic races patients to hospital with the siren on, Hooky tows in breakdowns, the post vans empty the postboxes, Sundae sells ice cream, the balloon flies scenic flights, the Duck runs duck tours, the blimp films the big match, and the airboat, yacht, Delta Belle, Defender and Mountain Goat each have their own.
  • Crop dusting. The Barnstormer flies three low passes along each field's rows. Too high and nothing lands on the crop.
  • The Juicebox minivan. Six seats, a sliding door, and a third more for the school run and soccer practice.
  • Medal times. Every delivery has bronze, silver and gold times, for up to +60% and a diamond.
  • Convoys. Moving Day, the Circus Comes to Town and a Relief Convoy, for fleets big enough to follow you in line.
  • Under the sea. Submarines dive on seabed sites for wreck finds, strongboxes and specimens, and the seabed has its own works: pearl beds, a research habitat, a drowned village, vent fields, a reef observatory and a listening post, with kelp farms and salmon pens at the surface.
  • Big loads. Ultra-Bulk work of 50 to 100 units for the biggest holds, gold long-haul jobs for jets and trains, and bands and league teams to carry to their next stadium.

🏭 Your buildings at work

  • Supply chains. Own a mill and it mills: nearly a hundred kinds of building turn their inputs into dearer goods, in twenty-five chains from timber to furniture and milk to ice cream. Goods made from your own goods are worth more at every step.
  • Creameries, binderies, wineries, joineries, smokehouses, roasteries, glassworks, cooperages, a luthier and more stand in the world near what they need, and works beside the railway (lumber sidings, coal loaders, tank farms, stockyards, inland ports, brewery sidings…) make and take goods for the trains.
  • Set a route of your own doors for a driver to run, or Fetch a load for any bin.
  • Blueprints. Own a building and you hold its plans: put up a copy in open country or a town you hold, in a style native to where it stands.
  • Buyouts. Buy any building off a rival firm, or the whole firm.

🛤️ Railways

  • Lay your own railway from the builder's Rails side, with halts, stations, termini, goods yards and harbour depots, double track, and lines across county borders and out over the sea on viaducts.
  • The trunk lines have a track each way, with island platforms at every station, so trains pass instead of waiting.
  • Hire train drivers with a rail licence and give them a line to run. Trains have a throttle lever and can change ends.

⏱️ Rushes

  • A hundred tiles from A to B against the clock: fill up in the ring at A, drop at B, and your followers shuttle beside you. Twenty kinds by road, sea, air and rail, each with a twist, and a board for every rush whose top fleet earns a diamond a day.

👑 Rewards

  • Gold for the Passport. Fill a page and it's sealed in gold with a treat: the Wonder Ball, Architect's Eye, the Explorer's lantern, the Blueprint collection and more. Fill the whole book for the Gilded Fleet.
  • Toppers and crowns. Over 240 roof ornaments to earn for your vehicles, and crowns held by the top fleets on every board, plus the world's records for highest, deepest, longest jump and longest delivery.
  • Fleet birthdays. Every game year on the day your fleet was founded, balloons float over everything it owns, and well-wishers send diamonds.
  • Dozens of new achievements, and faction Oaths that tell you where to go as soon as you reach 9,999.

👥 Playing together

  • Who's online. The online count in the corner pulses when someone comes on and says who. Click it for everyone online, with Follow.
  • The social window (I): Standings, a fleet card for every name, Neighbours, shared fleets, the World and an Inbox.
  • The Tribune, fresh every day with news from every fleet, and boards for every vehicle.
  • Play as guest, straight in with no name or PIN, and keep the fleet when you sign up.

🌤️ Everything else

  • Formation flypasts over stadiums and race meetings, a real balloon festival, real planes parked at the airports, and meteorites to bring home after a shower.
  • A first road to learn the keys on while the world loads, a gentle first drive with a Helping Hand who joins your fleet, and the controls tucked away until you want them.
  • Driver faces, a music player, a tech tree drawn as clear rows, and boost pads with their own tab in the builder.
  • Far-off land shades smoothly, and the freezes when opening the garage, the tech tree or the road builder are gone.

Thanks for playing, and for all the reports that shaped this one (the pause menu's Feedback button comes straight to me). See you on the new road! 🚗
